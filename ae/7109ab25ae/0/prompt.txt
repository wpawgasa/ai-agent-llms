<result>Answering whether blending text and voice metrics is the right call for a dual-modality model.

Yes — with three conditions, and one caveat that cuts against weighting voice too heavily *in selection specifically*.

**Why it's right:** the selection metric should reflect the deployment distribution. If the model serves both modalities, ranking on text alone can hand you a winner that's quietly bad at the half you actually ship to.

**Three conditions to make it sound:**

1. **Compute it as a weighted mean of per-modality means, not a mean over pooled rows.** These differ. Pooling rows makes the effective weight whatever the row count happens to be, so it drifts every time benchmark data is regenerated — and nobody notices, because the number still looks like a number. `quality = w·mean(voice) + (1−w)·mean(text)` is stable under regeneration; `mean(all_rows)` is not.

2. **Put `w` in `configs/benchmark/selection_weights.yaml`, not in the data mix.** As currently scoped, the 30% voice share *is* the weight — a product decision living in a generation ratio where nobody would think to look, and untunable without regenerating 1,000 conversations. Make it explicit and you can re-weight in seconds.

3. **Always report the per-modality split beside the blend.** Otherwise you can't tell whether a winner is better at workflow state machines or just better at emitting `&lt;S&gt;` tags. Those are different capabilities and one is much cheaper to install.

**The caveat, and it's the substantive one:** Phase 1 ranks *pre-trained* candidates that have never seen this convention. Voice score there is substantially a novel-instruction-following measurement — and that's precisely the skill SFT installs cheaply. R17 is the evidence: C2 took `tool_f1` on tool-bearing rows from 0.249 to 0.636 purely by fixing the training window, and R16 showed the voice-style convention was simply unlearnable from a 1024-token window until it wasn't. So a candidate that looks weak on voice at Phase 1 may not be weak post-SFT, while a candidate weak on *state-machine reasoning* probably still will be.

That argues for blending with voice weighted at or below its deployment share — 0.3 is sensible — rather than at the 0.5–0.7 that "voice is the real target" would suggest. Select on the capability that's expensive to teach; verify the one that's cheap to teach after fine-tuning, in the held-out audit that already reports voice separately.

Cost either way: changing the composite invalidates the existing gemma-4-26B-A4B selection for comparison purposes, so a meaningful new ranking needs Phase 1 re-run across the candidate set.</result>

<result>No — three benchmarks is the wrong shape. You want **two corpora, two inference passes, three reported numbers.**

"Blended" isn't a third benchmark, it's a derived statistic. If you generate a separate blended corpus and score it independently, its result won't equal the weighted mean of the other two — sampling noise guarantees it — so you'd own two disagreeing "blended" figures and no principled way to pick. And you'd pay 3× inference across 15 candidates for zero extra information, since the blend is arithmetic on numbers you already have.

The structure that gives you all three cheaply:

```
text corpus  ──► score ──► mean(text)   ─┐
                                          ├──► blended = w·voice + (1−w)·text
voice corpus ──► score ──► mean(voice)  ─┘
```

Text and voice are **strata of one evaluation**, not separate evaluations.

**And there's a stronger reason to keep the corpora separate than convenience** — one I just verified rather than assumed. Your on-disk benchmark is `gemini-3-flash-preview`-generated from June. Regenerating L1 at the same `seed=100` today produces a *different* corpus:

```
on-disk : 21394cafbcdef93b
regen   : 5dd66582ab884c06
```

Two causes, both real: that artifact was teacher-generated (the filename says so) while the DVC stage now runs placeholder-only, and commit `0ad770e` — the `PYTHONHASHSEED` fix from the voice branch — changed generator output by sorting a set before shuffling. So **regenerating the benchmark as a mixed corpus would silently discard the exact data the existing gemma-4-26B-A4B ranking was measured on.**

That makes the recommendation concrete:

1. **Freeze `data/output/benchmark/task_a` as-is.** Treat it as the text stratum. It stays byte-comparable to the existing Phase 1 ranking.
2. **Add `data/output/benchmark/task_a_voice` as an additive corpus** — the same pattern already used for the SFT corpus, and the same reason.
3. **Score both in one benchmark run**, group by modality, report `mean(text)`, `mean(voice)`, and the weighted blend with `w` living in `selection_weights.yaml`.

You get the blended ranking you asked for, plus a text number that still reconciles with the ranking you already have — which a regenerated mixed corpus would have quietly destroyed.
