<result># Recommendation: `response_only` for Cat A

**Confidence: high.** The token budget makes this close to unambiguous, and the measurement turned up a second, larger problem along the way.

## The decisive number

Per-role token share on 250 real `task_a_splits/train.jsonl` conversations, with the system prompt rebuilt exactly as training does it (`build_enriched_system_prompt`), tokenized with a Gemma-family SentencePiece tokenizer:

| role | tokens | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **system** | 744,479 | **71.4%** |
| assistant | 220,846 | 21.2% |
| user | 51,074 | 4.9% |
| tool | 25,789 | 2.5% |

Under `all_tokens`, **79% of gradient goes to tokens the model never emits at inference.** The system prompt is supplied in context. Customer turns come from the customer. Tool results come from the environment. Switching to `response_only` gives a **4.72× increase in gradient density** on the only tokens that are actually a prediction target.

That reframes the null results in `docs/cat_a_state_annotation_convention_review.md`. The 3.5×-epochs experiment (composite 0.6579, p=0.513) added compute but kept the same 21% allocation; `response_only` at 1× epochs delivers more assistant-token gradient than 3 epochs of `all_tokens` does.

One specific harm worth naming: 2.5% of gradient trains the model to *generate* `{"status": "success", ...}` payloads. That is literally training toward tool-result hallucination — the failure mode `corpus-remediator.md` and the R12/tool-emission-gap work exist to suppress. `response_only` masks it to `-100` for free.

## The bigger problem I found on the way

`configs/training/sft_cat_a.yaml:35` sets `max_seq_length: 4096`, and the frozen config for the run that produced weights (`checkpoints/sft_cat_a/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it/frozen_sft_config_20260722T051246Z.yaml`) confirms 4096 was used.

Measured conversation lengths: **median 4,264 tokens; p90 6,055; max 9,108.** The system prompt alone is a median 3,016.

**56% of training conversations exceed the 4096 window.** And the `all_tokens` path truncates *right* — `sft.py:674-675` passes `truncation=True, max_length=...`, and `truncation_side` is `right` for this tokenizer family. So on more than half the corpus, training silently discarded the **end** of the conversation: the terminal `[STATE: X → TERMINAL]` transitions, the final tool calls, the closing turns.

That is a plausible partial cause of the exact symptom under investigation. Task-completion rate and terminal-state accuracy are being measured against endings that over half the training examples never showed the model.

`render_response_only_sample` already gets this right — its docstring says *"Truncates from the left so the final assistant turn always survives"* (`sft.py:37-38`). So `response_only` fixes the truncation direction as a side effect. But **raise `max_seq_length` to 8192 regardless** — that covers 99% of conversations (2/250 exceed it) and the fix is orthogonal to the loss-mask choice. Do not treat left-truncation as a substitute for a window that fits.

## What I'd run

Three cells against the fixed v2 corpus, one variable at a time:

| cell | `loss_mask` | `max_seq_length` | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| C0 | `all_tokens` | 4096 | reproduce the existing baseline |
| C1 | `all_tokens` | 8192 | isolates the truncation effect alone |
| C2 | `response_only` | 8192 | the recommended recipe |

C1 matters — without it you cannot tell whether a C2 win came from the loss mask or from stopping the truncation, and those have different implications for everything downstream.

Give each cell an explicit `output_dir`. Per CLAUDE.md's R13 note, that key is the supported way to keep factorial cells from overwriting each other, and without it C1 silently lands on C0.

## Two things to watch

**`eval_loss` is not comparable across cells.** `sft.py:283-291` already emits an `sft_loss_mask_response_only` warning about this — the denominators differ (assistant tokens vs all non-pad tokens). Select on `metric_for_best_model` at your peril; compare cells on composite score / state-transition accuracy / tool-call F1 instead, and expect C2's absolute `eval_loss` to look *worse* while the task metrics improve.

**Effective batch shrinks in tokens-with-gradient.** At a fixed sequence budget, C2 backprops through ~21% as many tokens per step. If gradient noise rises visibly, raise `effective_batch_size` rather than the LR — with QLoRA on a 26B MoE, LR increases interact badly with the frozen router.

**Cost:** `response_only` pays O(N²) tokenization at dataset-prep time (per-turn incremental `apply_chat_template`). On 4,716 train examples that's a one-time cost of minutes, and `docs/fine_tuning_recipes.md:132` already documents it as the known trade.
