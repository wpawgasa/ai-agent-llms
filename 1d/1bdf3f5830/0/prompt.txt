The profiler reports large `SPEC-CONFORMANCE VIOLATIONS` counts. These are advisory, not defects, and split cleanly into two different causes that need different fixes.

| L5/travel | 157 | **100%** | 7 vs floor 7 | **Generator undershoot** |

| L4+L5 banking, insurance, L4/travel | 890 | **0%** | 7 / 8 / 7 | Compliant |

The L5/travel case is a precise, fixable undershoot: `travel` has 7 tools but the L5 subgraph selector emitted only 6 across **all 157 rows**, always omitting the same one — `file_travel_claim`. The disruption/claim branch that owns it is never selected into the L5 spine.

**`num_states` below floor: L5 279/750 (37.2%); L4 only 3/750 (0.4%).** The L4 result is essentially compliant. The L5 shortfall is largely structural — the profiler's ceiling notes fire for all five L5 domains:

```
⚑ L5/healthcare: canonical max is 16 states; the upper L5 target (20) is unreachable.
⚑ L5/banking:    canonical max is 17 states; the upper L5 target (20) is unreachable.
⚑ L5/telecom:    canonical max is 16 ...   ⚑ L5/travel: 17 ...   ⚑ L5/insurance: 16 ...
```

L5's `target_path_len` is `(16, 20)` but **no domain in the registry exceeds 17 states**. The upper bound is unreachable by construction; the lower bound of 16 is reachable only at the very top of each domain's range.

## 8. Recommendations, prioritized

1. **Merge both datasets.** No blocking defect. (No action on A or B for the text batch — it is clean.)
2. **Decide on the 109 voice placeholder rows before merging.** They are 3.63% of voice, carry `{"placeholder": "value"}` arguments, canned `[ตา N]` user turns, and filler speech `&lt;S&gt;Handling Insurance in state GREETING.&lt;/S&gt;`. They account for **100% of the voice tool-argument findings** (957 missing-required + 484 undeclared). `scripts/remove_placeholder_conversations.py` already exists. Since argument fidelity is the standing Cat A bottleneck (R17/R23: 409/528 mined errors are wrong-arguments), training on 109 rows of literal `"placeholder": "value"` is directly counterproductive. **Recommend dropping them.**
3. **Drop or repair the 2 defective teacher voice rows** — `l4_conversations_en_...20260831_160820.jsonl::L4_018` (CJK key `destination吸引`) and `l1_conversations_en_...20260831_054144.jsonl::L1_126` (enum violation). Two rows out of 2,891; trivial cost, and the CJK one teaches a malformed argument key.
4. **Record the behavior-preset decision explicitly.** The 1,500 text rows are 25.4% adversarial_probing, not 15%. If that is intended, note it where corpus composition is tracked; if not, regenerate with `--behavior-preset default`. Consider updating `.claude/rules/02-data-generation.md`, which documents only the default distribution while `scripts/generate_sft_data.sh:68` ships `adversarial` as its default — an easy trap for the next person.
5. **Generator fix (low priority, real):** the L5 subgraph selector never emits `file_travel_claim` for `travel` (157/157 rows), and L4/telecom lands below its reachable 6-tool floor in 84.1% of rows. Both are undershoots in the subgraph selector, not domain limits.
6. **Spec fix, not a data fix:** `COMPLEXITY_SPECS["L5"].target_path_len = (16, 20)` and `num_tools = 7` are **unsatisfiable** for healthcare and telecom (16 states, 6 tools each). Either lower the L5 floors to what the registry can support, or expand those two domains' canonical graphs. Until then, ~37% L5 spec violations are structural noise and re-running generation will not clear them.
